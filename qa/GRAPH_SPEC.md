# Spotify / CDs — provisional Clayton graph specification

Status: **three-source comparison completed 1 October 2026**. The user supplied `TOPIC 6A (1).pdf`. Its PDF page 20 reproduces the same lecturer template; pages 25–27 explain the arrows and demand levels. Proposed graph: `assets/spotify_clayton_graph.png`. Visual side-by-side review: `qa/side_by_side.png`.

- **Incumbent:** purchased compact discs / physical recorded music (focus on CDs; do not conflate the different fidelity of vinyl and CD).
- **Entrant:** Spotify on-demand streaming, launched October 2008 in six European countries.
- **Primary Y-axis metric:** fidelity of the delivered digital music signal relative to a lossless recording, at a supported connection and playback setup. This is a qualitative conceptual performance axis, **not numerical test data or perceived sound-quality rankings**. A standard CD carries 16-bit / 44.1 kHz PCM. Spotify initially offered compressed audio and introduced a lossless Premium option only in 2025 (up to 24-bit / 44.1 kHz FLAC). The graph cannot claim that every streamed song, free user or Bluetooth playback delivers lossless audio.
- **Why this fits Clayton, provisionally:** original CD signal was lossless, while early Spotify traded that conventional quality for instant catalogue access and low initial cost. The entrant's fidelity subsequently improved and eventually included lossless. **Tension:** CD format's digital fidelity remained substantially fixed; drawing a steeply rising incumbent line would imply an unsupported improvement. If the lecturer requires two similarly sloped rising lines, label CD quality as a high near-level trajectory, or reconsider the metric after seeing Topic 6A. Do not silently make up an increasing CD specification.
- **High-end demand:** critical listeners and collectors prioritising CD-standard lossless signal and ownership. This is a conceptual segment, not a claim that all CD buyers were audiophiles.
- **Low-end / underserved demand:** listeners willing to accept compressed playback in return for searchable, on-demand, low upfront-cost listening. This is a tolerance for lower *conventional audio fidelity*, not an income label.
- **Initial disadvantage:** lossy compressed stream compared with lossless CD signal; internet and licensing dependency treated separately, not blended into Y.
- **Different value / new performance trajectory:** instant discovery and broad on-demand access without buying individual discs. Annotate as an orthogonal callout, not a second hidden Y metric.
- **Verified years for X:** 2008 launch; 2009 mobile; 2013 first free mobile experience; 2015 Discover Weekly; 2017 global streaming format becomes the largest recorded-music revenue source; 2025 Premium Lossless and physical formats still present. Use only a few legible milestones on the graph, with other evidence in text.
- **Good enough:** mainstream expansion happened *before* 2025 lossless; do not place the low-end demand crossing at 2025 and imply fidelity parity caused streaming's market shift. Its exact date is not observable from these sources. If 2017 is marked as an observed industry revenue milestone, it must be distinct from a precise Y-axis threshold crossing.
- **Disruption claim:** streaming changed music distribution and access economics; IFPI's industry-wide streaming revenue is not a Spotify-only outcome or proof of a causal mechanism. Digital downloads and other streaming services also contributed.
- **Caveat:** strict classification as Christensen-style low-end disruption remains arguable. Spotify's early novelty was more convenient and accessible than CDs, while incumbent labels supplied music to the platform. CDs/vinyl remain in demand; physical revenue grew in 2025. Avoid saying Spotify alone destroyed physical music.

## Gate decision

**Pass on model structure, with a substantive caveat.** The proposal is immediately recognisable beside the lecturer's page 20: same TIME/PERFORMANCE axes, yellow upper CD path, green lower-to-upper Spotify path, red/purple horizontal demand arrows, blue alternative-value callout, and high/low customer labels. The PDF sample also places incumbent and entrant trajectories with audience levels and time along the lower edge. The CD yellow arrow is deliberately almost level, because its digital format did not keep increasing in resolution. The sharp green endpoint indicates the 2025 lossless option, not a claim that all Spotify playback is now better than CD. The 2017 industry revenue event is written along the time axis and explicitly **does not** mark a fidelity-parity crossing. The unmeasured low-end threshold crossing is illustrative, without a date claim. Do not describe the conceptual y positions as observed data.

The sample establishes layout logic only; its unsupported historical claims must not migrate into the new report.
